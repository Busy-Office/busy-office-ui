What this prototype is

This pattern is not in busy-office-ui. It is a proposal, built from the framework's real components and tokens so it can be judged in place. Everything it renders — alerts, badges, widget, buttons, form fields, icons — is existing framework markup; only the .rd* layout classes are new, and they use nothing but published tokens.

The problem it addresses: validation today answers one field at a time. A user learns that the cost center is closed, fixes it, saves, and then learns the amount exceeds their approval limit. Three round trips to discover three facts the server knew at the first request.

Four decisions worth arguing with:

  • Passed checks stay visible (collapsed, quiet). A list of only failures can't be trusted to be complete — showing what was checked is what makes the panel authoritative rather than advisory.
  • Blockers and warnings are separated by what they do, not by how bad they feel. A blocker stops the post; a warning does not. Nothing in between, because "sort of blocking" is what teaches users to ignore the panel.
  • Every entry says who can resolve it. "You can fix this" and "Needs Finance" are different user journeys, and conflating them is why approval chains stall — the user stares at a field they have no authority over.
  • The disabled action carries its reason inline. A greyed button with no explanation is the single most common ERP dead end.

Two-channel throughout, per the framework's rule: every severity carries a glyph and a word, never the tint alone.
