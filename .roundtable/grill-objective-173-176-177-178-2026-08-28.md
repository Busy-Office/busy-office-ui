## B. Slice 177's two "agreeing readings" are a ratio and its denominator, and the quantity that says what a wake pays moves the other way — **Evidence**

177's headline: *"Cycle length in commits is **halving** (140 → 66 → 33) while
the per-commit rate is **rising** (+30 → +51 → +68). **Both readings agree**,
which is what makes this worth a line: the sweep is not converging on a steady
state."* `LOOPS.md` rule 4 carried the same pair.

**They cannot disagree.** Rate × length = regrowth, identically. The two figures
are one measurement decomposed, so their "agreement" is arithmetic, not
corroboration — and the rate rises precisely *because* the length falls faster
than the total does.

Re-derived over all **725** commits that have touched `ROADMAP.md`, by the
line-count drop rather than by subject line. The fourth sweep (177.1 itself)
closed the cycle 177 could only measure mid-flight, so three cycles are now
complete:

| cycle | trough | peak | regrowth | commits | per commit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| after 110.4 (08-22)    | 5,562 | 9,824 | **4,262** | 141 | +30.2 |
| after tidy-44 (08-25)  | 1,094 | 4,461 | **3,367** |  67 | +50.3 |
| after 165's 20 (08-28) | 1,508 | 3,872 | **2,364** |  35 | +67.5 |
| after 177.1 (08-28)    | 2,030 | 2,194 |   164 |   1 | — *(n=1, open)* |

**The third column was never reported, and it falls: 4,262 → 3,367 → 2,364. So
does the peak a wake actually walks: 9,824 → 4,461 → 3,872.** Rule 4's cost is
the peak — it is the rule that walks the live file every wake — so on the number
that matters there, the sweep **is** converging, by a factor of 2.5 across three
cycles. "Not converging on a steady state" is the opposite of what the cost
series says.

**Cycle length is also endogenous, which the halving claim reads as growth.** A
cycle ends when a wake *notices*, and the trigger has fallen with it — swept at
9,824, then 4,461, then 3,872. Part of "the gap is halving" is wakes looking
sooner, not the file growing faster.

**Counter-evidence, and it keeps half of 177 standing.** The per-commit rate
rise is real and is not an artefact: each ROADMAP-touching commit really does
write 30 → 68 lines, and 177's own measurement of *what* generates it (61% of
the swept lines are Objective-grill slices that also have a full `.roundtable/`
report) is untouched by this. 177's one forward prediction also **held**: the
fourth sweep came 35 commits after the third, against 67 and 141. And its figures
reproduce — 141/67/35 against its 140/66/33, +30.2/+50.3/+67.5 against its
+30.4/+51.0/+67.9, the differences being the cycle-boundary convention plus cycle
3 having been measured at 33 commits and 3,750 lines before its own sweep closed
it. This corrects an interpretation, not a measurement.

**Filed as 179.2.** `LOOPS.md` rule 4 now carries the three-column table and the
decomposition; ROADMAP 177 carries a correction block in place rather than a
rewritten claim, so the original reading stays readable.
