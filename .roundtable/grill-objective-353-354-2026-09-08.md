## Defect 1 — Slice 354's halves table mixes two conventions in one row, and the wrong half is the heading number

**Published:** the observation half is *"quoted by **11 of 21** non-self
citations"* — in Slice 354's heading, in item `354.1`, and in Slice 327's
`327.3` closure note.

**Measured: 12 of 21.**

```

non-self (minus 327, the self-slice)                                          21
of those, quoting `shipped BESIDE`   12  [212, 215, 229, 232, 236, 265, 285, 298, 299, 319, 322, 330]
```

**It is not merely off by one — the table is internally impossible.** Every one
of the 12 observation-half slices cites 192.1, and 327 (the self-slice) does
not quote the observation half. So the citation column and the population
column count the **same set**, and the table prints them as **11** and **12**.

The two numbers come from two conventions, and exactly one slice separates
them:

```
observation half in the BODY      11  [212, 215, 229, 232, 236, 265, 285, 298, 299, 322, 330]
observation half in the HEADING    1  [319]        <- Slice 319 quotes it in its heading only
observation half anywhere         12
```

`11` is the body-only count; `12` is the whole-text count. The population
column's **12 (7.5%)** is the whole-text count, and the same needle over the
control corpus — **0 / 174**, the zero that licenses reading the table at all —
is also whole-text. So the citations column is the one that changed convention,
and it is the one that reached the heading.

**What it does not change:** the direction of the finding. 12 against 1 is the
same argument as 11 against 1, slightly stronger. The headline `1 of 161`
reproduces exactly, as does everything the base-rate section rests on. The
defect is in what shipped **beside** the headline measurement — which is the
rule Slice 354 was measuring compliance with, arriving inside the slice that
measures it.

**Corrected in place** in all three locations, with a correction block in Slice
354 naming this grill.

## This grill's own two instrument errors, published

CLAUDE.md's base rate arriving on schedule inside a grill about exactly this.

- **`git rev-parse --short origin/main HEAD` exited 128** at the pre-commit
  collision check — *"Needed a single revision"*. `ENVIRONMENT.md` §1 documents
  this verbatim, including the warning that it fails *"on every container,
  `main` present or not"*, so a wake reading the failure as a broken `main`
  would "fix" something that was never broken. The two-argument form without
  `--short` answered both shas. Cost: one command.
- **The verification of Defect 1's own fix was an assertion trippable by its
  own explanation.** `355.1`'s first draft claimed
  `grep -c '11 of 21'` → **3 → 0**. That is false as soon as the item says what
  it corrected: the removed string legitimately survives in the prose removing
  it, so the assertion cannot pass. This is CLAUDE.md's *"verifying a removal:
  assert on structure, never on raw text"* — written into a grill whose whole
  subject is numbers that are right at one revision and wrong at another. The
  structural form replaced it: name the three sites, assert each now reads
  `12`, and account for the survivors.

Neither reached a published number. Both are recorded because *"an instrument's
first output is not evidence"* is a base rate, not a warning.
