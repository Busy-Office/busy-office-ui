## The pattern: the defect is in the NOUN beside the number

All three defects are counts published beside work that is correct, which is
CLAUDE.md's 192.1 shape. The sharper reading is that in none of the three is the
*measurement* wrong — in each, the number is a faithful reading of a **different
population or quantity than the noun next to it names**:

| | the noun | what was counted |
|---|---|---|
| A | "top-level **sections**" | headings, `^#`, which includes the title |
| B | "the 10 **flagged pages**" | the corpus half of a corpus-∪-family definition |
| C | "the cost … **for twenty child processes**" | a superseded reading, of the total rather than the delta |

That is why review passed all three: check the arithmetic and each is
self-consistent, and in all three the **conclusion is unaffected** — 17 sections
still want auditing, the lane is still clean, executing the self-tests is still
cheap. The check that would have caught them is not arithmetic and not a gate;
it is reading the sentence and asking *which population does that noun name, and
did I count that one?*

Two of the three are also **inside their own commit's reach**: C's correction was
made in `ac4a9a0f` and not propagated, and A's 18 is one `grep -c` from 17. Only
B needed anything from outside the wake, and even that was printed in the
previous sweep's own entry.

**No gate is proposed for any of them.** *"The noun matches the population"* is
semantic, which is 94.11's wall. `336.2` proposes making the instrument print the
union so lane 3's noun and its number cannot diverge again — that is a report
change, not a gate, and it is filed as a question rather than built here.
