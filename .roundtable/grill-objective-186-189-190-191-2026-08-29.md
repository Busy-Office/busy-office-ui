## A. Every defect in 173.2 was in something ASSERTED; the one thing MEASURED was correct — **Evidence** (3 of 3)

Slice 190 found three defects in 173.2, which had shipped hours earlier in the
same session. Sorting them by how the shipping wake established each claim is
not a wash:

| what 173.2 did | how it was established | defect found next wake |
|---|---|---|
| row height 75px → 53px, siblings stop shifting | **measured live, both states, then red-proved** by re-injecting the flow message | **none** |
| `max-inline-size: 18ch` + `padding-block-end: 3.5rem` | **asserted** — both constants chosen against the single 21-character string the one demo carries | **190.1** — a 101-char message becomes a six-line 118px box and is clipped by 53px |
| the three new runtime claims | **skipped** — CLAUDE.md's recipe requires a `check:claims` case and none was added | **190.2** — gate read 141 before and after the commit |
| `(0,3,0 vs 0,2,0)` in a load-bearing comment | **asserted from reading the selectors** | **190.3** — the real numbers are (0,4,0) and (0,3,1), and (0,2,0) is a *third* rule's |

**Three for three.** The defect rate among *asserted* claims was 100%; among
*measured* ones, 0%. That is a sharper statement than "verify more", because it
names where to spend the next hour: 173.2's wake did do the hard measurement —
it built a browser harness, measured both states, and red-proved the result —
and then shipped three claims beside it that nobody measured at all.

**The two constants are the most instructive.** `18ch` and `3.5rem` look like
design decisions and were in fact *fits to a sample of one*. 190.1's control
shows the cost precisely: neutralising 173.2's three rules, the same 101-char
message is **36px on one line** and clipped by nothing; with them it is **118px
over six lines**. The cap, added to make the box tidy, is what overruns the
reserve added to make the box fit.

**Counter-evidence, and it is substantial.** All three were caught within one
wake by a routine that exists for exactly this, none reached a consumer, and
190.1's severity was *downgraded by its own measurement* after being drafted as
a P0 (the clipped region scrolls; `aria-describedby` carries the full string;
**1 of 138** built pages nests such a message). A process that surfaces three
defects in fresh work inside a day is not obviously failing.

## B. A gate written to catch a mistake caught the wake writing it, twice in one slice — **Evidence**

190.2's own construction produced two instrument defects, and both were caught
by assertions rather than by review:

- `getComputedStyle` returns a **live** object. The counterfactual read it
  *after* removing the injected style, so the snapshot reverted and the case
  reported `injectionLanded: false` on a mutation that plainly worked
  (`inFlow 103 > fixed 53`).
- The red-proof's injection targeted `ls dist/_astro/*.css | head -1`, which is
  the first file **alphabetically**, not the one holding the rule. The
  `assert count > 0` is the only reason this was noticed; without it the
  red-proof returns green and reads as success.

The second is CLAUDE.md's documented minified-output trap arriving again, and
it is the **third** time in this session an injection assertion has paid for
itself (the others: the `2000px` spacer count in 173.1, and the `stripTags`
byte-diff in 187).

**Counter-evidence**: both were caught *by the discipline working as written*,
which argues the rule is effective rather than that the base rate is alarming.
The honest reading is the one CLAUDE.md already states — a green red-proof is a
defect in the injection until proven otherwise — and this window is three more
data points for it, not a new finding.

## C. The window's own shape, stated without re-raising the retired ratio — **Hypothesis**

Three of four slices are grills or sweeps. The tempting inference is retired and
is not drawn here. What *is* new and checkable: **190 is the first slice in this
window whose findings were entirely about consumer-facing shipped CSS**, and it
was produced by a grill. So the self-auditing lane produced the window's only
product findings — which is evidence *against* reading grill-heavy windows as
off-direction, not for it.

**Counter-evidence, and it is why this is a Hypothesis**: one instance. And
190's findings exist only because 173.2 shipped product code in the first place
— a window with no product work would give the grills nothing to find. The two
lanes are not independent, so a ratio between them (retired, correctly) would
have measured nothing anyway.
