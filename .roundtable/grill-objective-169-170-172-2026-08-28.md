## E. Step 0c's own reopen condition fired on this very collision — **Evidence**

Not a finding this grill went looking for; it happened to it.

`LOOPS.md` Step 0c accepts collisions on a stated safety argument: *"Two
concurrent wakes therefore collide in those two files even when their code
changes are disjoint: the loser's rebase conflicts, so it cannot land silently on
top of work it never read."* And it names its own trigger: *"What would reopen
this: a collision that LANDS rather than being rejected — two wakes whose only
overlap was the append point of `loop-log.md` and whose rebase resolved
cleanly."*

**The rebase resolved cleanly.** `git pull --rebase` replayed all three commits
with no conflict at all.

**Stated exactly, because the trigger's wording is narrower than what happened.**
Step 0c anticipated a collision whose *only* overlap was `loop-log.md`'s append
point. This one overlapped on two other files — `ROADMAP.md` and `LOOPS.md` —
and still merged clean, while `loop-log.md` was not in the loser's diff at all.
So it is not the literal condition; it is the same failure through a wider door,
and the safety claim the condition exists to protect is the one that broke.

```
git diff --name-only 5f211132 7cc6e73b   # the other wake:  9 files
git diff --name-only 7cc6e73b 6c2ce5e0   # this wake:       4 files
comm -12 …                               # overlap: LOOPS.md, ROADMAP.md
```

Both named collision points failed, for different reasons:

- **`loop-log.md` — not touched by the loser at all.** `record_iteration.py`
  runs *after* the commit, once per wake, so for nearly the whole of a wake the
  append point is untouched. This wake rebased before recording, which is the
  normal ordering the playbook prescribes, so the file that guarantees a
  conflict was not in the loser's diff. The guarantee holds only for a wake that
  has already recorded when the other pushes — the minority of the wake.
- **`ROADMAP.md` — touched by both, merged cleanly.** Two wakes ticking boxes in
  *different* slices produce disjoint hunks. They closed 170.3; this wake
  renumbered a Slice 172 heading and appended Slice 175, ~400 lines away.

So the mechanism that was supposed to make a silent land impossible did not
fire, and what actually caught the other wake was the **`git fetch origin main`
before the first commit** that the same section mandates as "the one thing that
changes, and it costs no push". That is the part that worked, and it is a
process rule with nothing mechanical behind it — a wake that skips it gets no
second signal.

**Counter-evidence, and it matters for severity.** Nothing was lost or
corrupted, and the two grills share no finding, so redundant coverage paid for
itself exactly as Step 0c's third refusal argues. The cost was one re-verification
pass, not one wake's work.

**The factual claim in Step 0c is corrected in place; the DECISION is left
open** as 175.4 — whether to keep accepting collisions is a direction question,
and direction has been the owner's in every slice so far. Recording a wrong
safety argument as if it still held would be the worse outcome either way.
