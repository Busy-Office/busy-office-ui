## Finding — 244.3 publishes two counts of one set, and neither matches the tree

`ROADMAP.md:436` says `_common.py` is *"imported by six other loop scripts"*.
`ROADMAP.md:446` says *"all **eight** other `_common` consumers still import"*.
They are counts of the same set, they disagree with each other, and neither is
what the tree holds.

```
for f in $(git ls-tree --name-only <rev> scripts/loops/ | grep '\.py$' | grep -v '_common.py'); do
  git show $rev:$f | grep -q "from _common import" && echo $f; done | wc -l
#   71a61679  (before 244.3)  -> 5
#   1590bc2b  (after)         -> 7
#   b0b70f96  (the tree the item was written against) -> 7
```

**Five before, seven after.** "Other than the two the item folded" is five. The
published six is reachable only in the intermediate state, after the first of
the two was routed and before the second — a figure read from the working tree
mid-edit, which is the exact form `ENVIRONMENT.md`'s standing trap warns about.

**The eight is a count of a different noun.** `scripts/loops` holds ten `.py`
files besides `_common.py`; minus the two this item changed, **eight**. That is
the number — but three of those eight (`generate_roundtable_index.py`,
`polish_requeue.py`, `report_loop_prose.py`) do not import `_common` at all, so
they are not *"`_common` consumers"*. The set was counted correctly and labelled
wrongly.

**The substance stands and is not reopened.** The consolidation is sound: all
seven real consumers import cleanly at `ff28dfdb`, and the four output
comparisons plus the two discrimination red-proofs the item records are about
`from_disk`/`from_rev`, not about how many callers exist. This is 238.1's shape
exactly — *the premise reproduces; what fails is the number quoted to size it* —
and it is the second consecutive grill to find one, which is why it is filed
rather than fixed in passing.

**Where it stands:** two prose sites, `ROADMAP.md:436` and `:446`. The
`loop-log.md:1305` row and its `STATUS.md:45` mirror carry the eight as well and
are **not** to be edited — `record_iteration.py`'s standing rule, upheld by
238.1 for the same class of error.
