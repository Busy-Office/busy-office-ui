# Objective grill — Slices 149, 150, 152 (2026-08-27)

Dispatched by LOOPS.md rule 3 at 3/3. Second Objective round of the day; the
first (`grill-objective-130-148`) covered seventeen slices and produced the
reach finding that Slice 150 acted on. This round grills that action.

---

## Finding 1 — zero reach has FOUR meanings, and 6 of 7 zeros are correct

Slice 150 shipped `report-reach.mjs` as a **report, not a gate**, on the
argument that zero reach has three meanings and a gate "would be wrong roughly a
third of the time". That estimate was made from three diagnosed components.

Diagnosing the two the report surfaced but nobody had examined — plus a recount
of the rest — makes the argument **much stronger than it was stated**, and adds a
category the first grill did not have.

| block | verdict | evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bo-tree` | **correctly refused at the point of use** | its own opener says *"not for rows that carry data columns"* and points at `tree-table`, which is used; every hierarchy in the suite carries data columns |
| `bo-avatar-stack` | **correctly refused**, and strongly | the suite HAS the exact scenario the primitive was promoted for — its CSS comment reads *"approval-chain 'who's next' stack — the scenario that promoted the primitive"* — and `p2p/purchase-order` renders that chain as a `bo-timeline` with named steps, `data-state` done/current/pending, timestamps and "waiting 2 days". Overlapping discs would lose every one of those. |
| `bo-toast-region` | **cannot appear — a NEW fourth meaning** | it is a *runtime container*: an app renders it once and injects toasts into it. A static screen has nothing to inject, so it can never appear in a composition corpus. Not a defect, and not "refused" either — structurally invisible to this measurement. |
| `bo-date` | **reached-for failure** — the real defect | 21 of 27 suite screens render a date; none uses it |
| `bo-file-dropzone` / `__input` / `__list` | **instrument gap** | no suite screen has an attachment flow at all; says nothing about the component |

**One of seven blocks is an actual framework problem.** A gate on zero reach
would have been wrong **six times out of seven** — far worse than the "a third
of the time" the slice estimated when it refused to gate.

That is worth stating precisely because the decision could easily have gone the
other way: principle 3 is written in the Objective ("nothing ships for one
screen"), the violation was real, and gating it would have looked like rigour.
It would have produced a red build that was wrong 86% of the time.

**The fourth meaning is the actionable part.** `report-reach` should say that a
runtime container cannot appear in the corpus, rather than listing it beside
`bo-date` as though they are the same kind of finding. Queued as **153.1**.

## Finding 2 — an instrument produced a 37.7% alarm that died to one question

Setting out to measure this project's refusal rate, the first query against
`loops.db` reported **356 of 944 rows (37.7%) carry an outcome outside the
enforced six-word vocabulary**, some of them entire sentences in the `outcome`
column.

That number is real and almost entirely meaningless:

- **139 of the 356 are `shipped`** — a vocabulary CLAUDE.md *already documents as
  rejected* ("shipped is rejected — it hid that nothing had reached npm").
  Superseded, not corrupt.
- **The newest offending row is 2026-08-19.** Nothing has been written outside
  the vocabulary since it was enforced. Not an active leak.
- **The log carries the same values**, so `rebuild_from_log.py` is faithful. The
  mirror is not lying about its source; the source is simply older than the rule.
- **Nothing queries `outcome` to make a decision.** `generate_status.py` reads it
  only to print it back.
- The one risk worth testing — that a group-by would silently undercount
  refusals — was tested and is **1 row out of 167**.

So the honest verdict is *"a documented vocabulary change, faithfully recorded,
with no decision impact"*, and the alarming version of it would have been a
wasted slice. Recorded because the failure mode is the one CLAUDE.md names
first: a tidy, alarming number from a fresh query is wrong until asked one
adversarial question. **This is the second time today** an instrument's first
output was misleading — the first was the reconciliation command that reported
zero uses of `bo-data-table`.

## Finding 3 — the refusal rate is 33%, and that reads as healthy

In the window where the vocabulary is reliable (≥ 2026-08-19, 584 rows):

| outcome | n |
| --- | --- |
| landed | 334 |
| refused | 166 |
| triaged | 41 |
| logged | 33 |
| released | 3 |

**166 refusals against 334 landed — a third of substantive outcomes are
refusals.** The Objective says refusing is a valid outcome; this is evidence the
loop actually behaves that way rather than only saying so.

Counter-evidence considered: a high refusal rate could mean the input channels
are producing weak candidates rather than that the filter is good. Slices
149-152 argue against that reading — the refusals were mostly of *mechanisms
that exist elsewhere and do not fit here* (a data-type column-width table
serving a virtualized grid, a filter surface where two already exist, a
`--priority` modifier serving one scenario), which is the filter doing its job on
reasonable input, not rejecting noise.

## Grading 149, 150 and 152

**Principle 1, Simplicity — holding.** 152 is the clearest case: the layout page
now shows the four shells as copyable templates *and* hand-maintains less prose
than before, because one array replaced two hand-written tables.

**Principle 2, Less for more — holding.** 149.4 answered one WCAG requirement two
different ways with **zero new CSS** rather than shipping a `--live` modifier;
149.1 adopted `bo-progress` once and refused it three times, on three distinct
reasons.

**Principle 3, Reusability — the picture improved, and the number did not
change.** The zero-reach count is still 7, but six of those are now positively
diagnosed as correct. The single defect is `bo-date`.

## Proposed

1. **153.1 — teach `report-reach` the fourth meaning.** Runtime containers
   (`bo-toast-region`) cannot appear in a static composition corpus; the report
   should say so rather than listing them beside real misses. Small, and it stops
   the number being misread by the next reader — the same class of fix as this
   morning's `scan-dead-style` wording.
2. **153.2 — `bo-date` is the one real miss.** 21 of 27 screens render a date as
   a plain string. Fixing the *screens* is the work; the component needs nothing.

## Refused

- **Gating zero reach** — re-refused with a much stronger number: 6 of 7 wrong.
- **Backfilling the pre-2026-08-19 outcome vocabulary.** It would rewrite
  historical log lines to satisfy a rule that did not exist when they were
  written, to fix a measurement error of 1 row in 167. The log is the reviewed
  record; editing history to tidy a mirror inverts the doctrine.
