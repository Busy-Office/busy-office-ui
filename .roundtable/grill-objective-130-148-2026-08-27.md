# Objective grill — Slices 112, 130-148 (2026-08-27)

Dispatched by LOOPS.md rule 3, **overdue at 17/3**. Seventeen slices closed since
the last Objective round on 2026-08-23: 112, 130, 131, 132, 133, 134, 135, 136,
139, 140, 142, 143, 144, 145, 146, 147, 148.

Graded against the Objective's three principles. The job of this round is to
find a **pattern across the seventeen**, not to restate one slice.

---

## The finding: the instrument is blind in one direction

**The ERP suite finds what the framework CANNOT express. It is structurally
blind to what the framework CAN express but nobody reaches for.**

Every one of the **21 gaps** the suite has produced is of the first kind — *"this
screen cannot be built from shipped CSS"*. That is what its zero-CSS gate
detects, and the gate works. But a screen that hand-rolls something the
framework already ships **passes that gate perfectly**, because hand-rolling
uses existing classes. The instrument cannot see it by construction.

### Evidence (two independent components, same shape)

| component | ships | reach across 27 suite screens | what the screens do instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bo-progress` | native `<progress>`, value/max, `--warning`/`--danger` documented as *"threshold states (approaching / over budget)"* | **1 of 27** | four screens hand-roll a threshold: `bucket()` tone buckets, a `short = t < rop` boolean, two unrelated `bo-kv` rows, a `Credit hold` badge |
| `bo-date` | the read-only Amount/Quantity counterpart for dates | **0 of 27**, while **21 of 27 render a date** | plain strings — `'01 Oct'` |

Two components, discovered on different days by different routes, with the same
signature. That clears this playbook's evidence gate (≥2 independent sources);
one alone would have been a Hypothesis.

### This directly fails Objective principle 3

> *Nothing ships for one screen. A piece earns its place by surviving ≥2 real,
> independent compositions.*

Three components have **never been composed into a screen or a pattern** — they
exist only on their own documentation page:

| component | suite | patterns | appears only in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bo-date` | 0 | 0 | `components/date.astro`, `components/amount.astro` |
| `bo-tree` | 0 | 0 | `components/tree.astro` |
| `bo-file-dropzone` / `__input` / `__list` | 0 | 0 | its own page |

The bar was written to stop things shipping for one screen. These shipped for
**zero**, and nothing noticed, because no gate asserts reach.

---

## But the three zeros have DIFFERENT causes, and conflating them would be wrong

This is the part a single number would have hidden.

- **`bo-date` — reached-for failure.** 21 screens show a date; none uses it. The
  screen could have, and did not. This is the `bo-progress` shape: a
  discoverability defect, and the framework's problem.
- **`bo-tree` — shape absence, correctly refused at the point of use.** Its own
  opener says *"Not for rows that carry data columns — the moment each node
  needs amounts, statuses or dates beside it, a list of links is the wrong
  choice"*, pointing at `tree-table`, which **is** used (2 screens). Every
  hierarchy in the suite carries data columns, so `tree-table` correctly wins
  every time. The component is fine; the suite simply has no pure-navigation
  hierarchy screen. That is the owner's **suitability-beats-reuse** precedence
  working exactly as written.
- **`bo-file-upload` — instrument coverage gap.** No suite screen has an
  attachment flow at all. Attachments are a real ERP job (a PO with a signed
  quote, a claim with photos); 27 screens across 6 modules contain none. That is
  a hole in the *instrument*, not evidence about the component.

**So "unused" carries at least three meanings, and only the first is a defect in
the framework.** A gate that simply failed on zero-reach would have flagged
`bo-tree` — which is behaving correctly — and taught people to work around it.

## Grading the seventeen slices against the three principles

**Principle 1, Simplicity — holding.** 147/148 pushed in the right direction:
`npm create @busy-office/ui` lets a consumer delete a whole assembly step, and
`check:quickstart` proves the documented path rather than describing it. The
`bo-progress` finding is a simplicity failure of a subtler kind — the thing is
simple, but four authors did not find it.

**Principle 2, Less for more — holding, with one watch item.** The strongest
recent evidence is 149.4: the same WCAG requirement produced *two different*
answers (a `bo-segmented` control on `job-monitor`, a settings preference for
`notification`) with **zero new CSS**, rather than a `--live` modifier. That is
the principle working. Watch item: `tree` and `tree-table` are two surfaces
adjacent to each other — currently justified by a clear wrong-choice clause, but
this is exactly the *Rethink* trigger and should be re-examined if a third
hierarchy surface ever appears.

**Principle 3, Reusability — FAILING, measurably.** Three components at zero
reach, and the bar that forbids it is stated in the Objective itself. This is the
round's finding.

---

## What is proposed, and what is deliberately refused

**Proposed — a reach measurement, reported, not gated.** Count each component's
independent compositions across the suite and patterns, and print the low end on
every build. **Refused as a hard gate**, for the reason above: zero reach is not
by itself a defect (`bo-tree`), so a red build would be wrong a third of the
time, and a gate that is wrong teaches evasion. Report the number; let a human
read the three meanings.

**Refused — building anything for the three zeros.** No new component is
implied. `bo-date` needs the *screens* changed, not the component; `bo-tree`
needs nothing; `bo-file-upload` needs a suite screen that has an attachment, and
that is a suite decision, not a framework one.

**Refused — a "usage" rubric dimension.** This is the `ux`-dimension mistake
already corrected once (145.x) and the entropy-report mistake refused in 148:
a property that is either true or false belongs in a measurement or a gate, not
scored on a scale.

## Instrument honesty

The measurement behind this grill was **wrong twice before it was right**,
consistent with the base rate this project has recorded:

1. Counting all of a component's `blocks` inflated results — `richtext` scored
   58 because `api.json` lists `bo-btn` among its blocks. **`blocks` is not an
   ownership map**; it lists every block a component's CSS mentions.
2. Restricting to blocks *unique* to one component inverted the bias — `button`
   dropped to 4, which is `bo-btn-group`'s reach, not button's.

Both were caught by the instrument's own printed checks (shared-block detection,
prefix-clash detection, spread/zero/max tests) before any number was quoted.
What survives **both** framings is the low end, and that is what is reported
here — the three zeros were then hand-verified with correct block names and
word-boundary matching.

A third error was in the *hand-check itself*, which is worth recording because it
inverts the usual story: a plain `grep bo-tree` matched `bo-tree-table`, and
`grep bo-file-upload` searched a class name that does not exist (the real blocks
are `bo-file-dropzone`/`__input`/`__list`). **The instrument was more precise
than the manual check used to audit it** — derive names from the generated
source, never from the component's name.
