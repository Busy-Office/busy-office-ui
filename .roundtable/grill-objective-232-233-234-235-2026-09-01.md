## §4 — Finding 2 (filed as 236.2): the sweep archived the target of an open item's Accept

`234.1` is **open** and its Accept (a) names 229.3's `RECURRENCE HISTORY` as a
thing to amend. 235.2's sweep moved Slice 229. That text now exists in exactly
one place:

```

ROADMAP-archive.md:30520     — the section itself, the only copy
```

So an open criterion is half-satisfiable in the live file (232.2's closing text,
`ROADMAP.md:1058`) and half only by editing the archive — the file `LOOPS.md`
rule 4 calls the place "for looking a reason UP", from which "a dispatch
decision never comes".

**235.2 could not have known.** Its five criteria — byte-identity, two-way line
accounting, citation neutrality, a raw open-checkbox count, target derivation at
move time — all passed and were all re-verified above. Every one is a property
of the *move*. None asks whether anything still open **depends on** the moved
text, and `check:slice-refs` cannot answer it either: it asks whether a citation
**resolves**, and `229.3` resolves fine from the archive. Resolving is the wrong
question when the criterion says *amend*.

**Base rate, measured before proposing anything (94.11).** Five open items;
three owner- or hardware-blocked. Of the two dispatchable, **234.1 needs
archived text edited and 232.3 does not** — 232.3 cites 230.1, also archived,
but its Accept is entirely about building and red-proving a detector, so a
citation that resolves is all it needs. **1 of 2.** A real rate on a
two-item denominator, reported as such.

**No gate proposed, and the refusal is measured.** The predicate a gate needs —
*does an open item's Accept require amending a section this sweep will move?* —
turns on the difference between a slice cited as a *reason* and one cited as a
*target*, which is semantic, the same line 94.11 draws. The checkable shape —
"an open item names a slice number whose body is archive-only" — fires on 232.3
too, where the behaviour is correct, so a gate over it would be red on a healthy
state. What is cheap and exact is a **sweep-time report** from
`roadmap_scope.py`, which already parses both the checkboxes and the headings.

**And the premise may be false, which 236.2's Accept (d) makes a satisfying
outcome.** Editing an archived section for a correction may simply be fine — the
archive is markdown, reviewed and diffed, and 235.3 already deleted from it. The
defect is not obviously that the archive is untouchable; it is that **no
document says either way**, so the next wake to reach 234.1 has to invent the
answer.
